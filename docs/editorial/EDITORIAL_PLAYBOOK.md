# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this document is to establish a unified editorial system for all current and future public content published on the Mall Agro platform.

Rather than acting as a collection of writing rules, this playbook defines the editorial philosophy, communication standards, structural principles and quality requirements that govern how Mall Agro presents itself as an engineering-driven B2B industrial platform.

This document serves as the editorial source of truth for all contributors involved in content creation, including human editors, AI assistants and future project collaborators.

Its objectives are to:

* maintain a consistent editorial identity across the entire website;
* ensure that all content supports the strategic positioning of Mall Agro;
* prevent generic catalogue-style writing;
* improve industrial credibility through operationally meaningful content;
* establish repeatable page structures and editorial workflows;
* protect the project from unsupported claims and inconsistent messaging;
* provide long-term guidance for future platform expansion.

This playbook applies to every publicly visible content element, including homepage sections, category pages, solution pages, equipment pages, landing pages, calls-to-action, metadata and supporting content.

# 2. Editorial Mission

Mall Agro does not exist to publish product descriptions.

Its purpose is to help industrial buyers understand operational requirements before equipment selection begins.

Every page should improve the visitor's understanding of an operational process, equipment role or project context.

The website should gradually move visitors from:

> operational challenge

to

> process understanding

to

> equipment direction

to

> structured commercial discussion.

The content itself therefore becomes part of the consultation process.

---

# 3. Editorial Philosophy

The editorial philosophy of Mall Agro is based on a simple principle:

> Explain the operation before explaining the equipment.

Industrial equipment has value only within the operational environment where it performs a specific role.

Visitors rarely begin with certainty about a particular machine.

Instead, they usually begin with questions such as:

* How should this process work?
* Which stage creates the bottleneck?
* What information is needed before selecting equipment?
* Which equipment family is relevant?
* How do different process stages connect?

Mall Agro should therefore communicate operational understanding first and equipment second.

The objective is not to impress visitors with technical complexity.

The objective is to build confidence that Mall Agro understands the industrial process behind every equipment discussion.

This philosophy distinguishes Mall Agro from generic online catalogues, product aggregators and equipment resellers.

# 5. Core Editorial Principles

All editorial decisions should comply with the following principles.

## Principle 1 — Operation Before Equipment

Always explain the operational context before introducing machinery.

Visitors should understand *why* equipment is needed before learning *what* equipment may be appropriate.

---

## Principle 2 — Process Before Product

Describe workflows, production stages and operational dependencies before discussing equipment families.

Products should appear as logical components of a broader industrial process.

---

## Principle 3 — Consultation Before Quotation

The website should encourage structured project discussions rather than immediate quotation requests.

Calls-to-action should invite meaningful conversations supported by operational information.

---

## Principle 4 — Clarity Before Complexity

Industrial terminology should improve understanding, not create barriers.

Content should remain accessible to business owners while maintaining credibility for engineers and technical decision-makers.

---

## Principle 5 — Information Before Marketing

Every paragraph should provide practical value.

If a sentence does not increase the reader's understanding of the operation, it should be rewritten or removed.

---

## Principle 6 — Credibility Before Promotion

The platform must communicate confidence without exaggeration.

Editorial quality is measured by clarity, usefulness and consistency rather than promotional language.

# 9. Writing Principles

Every piece of public content should follow these editorial principles.

## Explain Before Selling

Visitors should understand the operational challenge before reading about equipment.

The role of the website is to improve understanding first and support commercial discussion second.

---

## Guide Rather Than Promote

The content should help visitors evaluate their own operational requirements.

Rather than encouraging immediate purchasing decisions, the website should guide readers toward a better understanding of their production context.

---

## Build Understanding Progressively

Each section should naturally prepare the reader for the next.

Information should progress from:

* operational context;
* production workflow;
* equipment role;
* selection considerations;
* project discussion;
* inquiry preparation.

Avoid introducing machinery before explaining where it fits within the overall process.

---

## Focus on Decisions

Industrial buyers rarely search for machines alone.

They search for answers to practical questions.

Examples include:

* Which production stage creates the bottleneck?
* What information is required before selecting equipment?
* Which operational factors influence equipment choice?
* How will new equipment interact with existing workflows?
* What should be clarified before requesting a proposal?

Content should answer these questions naturally throughout the page.

---

# 10. Information Density

Information density is one of the most important quality indicators for Mall Agro content.

Every paragraph should introduce new knowledge.

Avoid repeating the same concept using different wording.

Poor example:

> Equipment should be selected carefully according to operational requirements.

Better example:

> Equipment selection depends on material characteristics, production capacity, workflow sequence, available space and integration with upstream and downstream process stages.

The second example provides practical information rather than abstract advice.

Before approving any paragraph, ask:

> Does the reader know something now that they did not know before reading this sentence?

If the answer is no, the paragraph should be rewritten.

> A paragraph is valuable only if it accomplishes at least one of the following:

- teaches something;
- clarifies a process;
- reduces uncertainty;
- explains an engineering relationship;
- prepares a business decision;
- improves operational understanding.

---

# 11. Preferred Writing Style

Use short, precise and well-structured sentences.

Prefer clear explanations over complex wording.

Use active voice whenever practical.

Introduce technical terminology only when it improves understanding.

Avoid unnecessary adjectives.

Avoid empty introductory phrases.

Avoid filler paragraphs.

Use bullet lists when presenting operational factors, production stages or decision criteria.

Paragraphs should normally contain one central idea.

Long blocks of uninterrupted text should be avoided unless the subject requires continuous explanation.

---

# 12. Forbidden Writing Patterns

The following writing patterns should not appear in Mall Agro content unless there is a very specific editorial justification.

Avoid generic promotional phrases such as:

* High-quality equipment
* Premium solutions
* Innovative technologies
* World-class products
* Complete solutions
* State-of-the-art machinery
* Leading supplier
* Best equipment
* Reliable partner
* Trusted company
* One-stop solution
* Cutting-edge technology

Avoid vague statements including:

* We understand your needs.
* We provide the best solutions.
* We deliver complete projects.
* We offer everything you need.
* We satisfy every customer requirement.

These statements provide no measurable value and reduce the perceived credibility of the website.

Replace abstract claims with operational explanations.

For example:

Instead of:

> We offer complete industrial solutions.

Prefer:

> Equipment discussions begin with understanding production objectives, workflow constraints and the role each process stage plays within the overall operation.

# 15. Category Page Blueprint

Category pages are not product listings.

They are Operational Knowledge Pages.

Their purpose is to explain an operational area before introducing equipment families.

Every category page should answer the following questions:

## What operational area does this category cover?

Explain the production environment.

Describe the activities involved.

Define the operational objective.

---

## Why is this operational area important?

Explain where it fits within agricultural or industrial workflows.

Describe typical operational challenges.

Identify common dependencies.

---

## Which workflows are involved?

Explain the sequence of operations.

Describe upstream and downstream relationships.

Help visitors understand how the operation connects with the wider production process.

---

## What role does equipment perform?

Do not begin by listing machines.

Instead explain:

* what equipment accomplishes;
* why different equipment types exist;
* which operational factors influence equipment selection.

Equipment should always appear as part of the process rather than the focus of the page.

---

## What information affects equipment selection?

Visitors should understand which project details matter before discussing machinery.

Examples include:

* material characteristics;
* production objectives;
* available space;
* workflow sequence;
* seasonal factors;
* existing equipment;
* production capacity;
* future expansion plans.

---

## What should happen next?

The page should naturally transition into a structured commercial discussion.

Calls-to-action should invite visitors to describe their operational context rather than request equipment immediately.

1. Positioning
2. Operational Focus
3. Equipment Categories

# 18. Calls to Action

Calls-to-action should feel like the natural conclusion of the discussion.

Visitors should never feel pressured.

Avoid aggressive sales language.

Poor examples:

* Buy Now
* Contact Us Today
* Request Your Machine
* Order Equipment

Preferred alternatives include:

* Discuss Your Project
* Describe Your Operational Requirements
* Start an Equipment Discussion
* Explore Suitable Equipment Directions
* Request a Project Consultation

The CTA should continue the conversation rather than close a sale.

# 20. Content Depth

Mall Agro should avoid both extremes:

Too little information creates generic marketing content.

Too much information creates technical documentation.

The preferred balance is:

* enough operational detail to establish credibility;
* enough clarity for non-specialists;
* enough structure to support decision-making;
* enough technical terminology to sound authoritative without becoming difficult to read.

Every page should answer practical questions without attempting to become an engineering manual.

Readers should leave each page with a clearer understanding of the operational context and with greater confidence to begin a structured equipment discussion.

# 21. Claim Safety Framework

Claim safety is a fundamental editorial principle throughout the Mall Agro platform.

Public-facing content must communicate confidence without creating unsupported commercial, technical or legal expectations.

Editorial quality is measured not only by what is written, but also by what is intentionally omitted.

Every public statement should remain defensible if reviewed by customers, partners or legal representatives.

# 22. SEO and AEO Writing Standards

Search visibility should be achieved through editorial quality rather than keyword repetition.

The objective is to create pages that answer practical industrial questions while remaining valuable to human readers.

Every page should naturally cover the entities, concepts and operational relationships associated with its subject.

---

Before writing any page, identify the primary visitor intent.

Examples include:

* understanding a production process;
* evaluating equipment categories;
* comparing operational approaches;
* preparing for an equipment discussion;
* researching workflow improvements.

The page should satisfy this intent before introducing commercial messaging.

* intake;
* cleaning;
* conveying;
* elevators;
* transfer points;
* storage;
* material flow;
* production sequence.

Every page should answer questions that industrial buyers naturally ask.

Examples include:

* What is this operational area?
* Why is it important?
* Which production stages are involved?
* Which operational factors affect equipment selection?
* What information is needed before discussing machinery?

---

Do not create paragraphs that exist only to repeat keywords.

Every paragraph should introduce useful knowledge.

# 25. Definition of Editorial Done

Content should be considered complete only when all of the following conditions are satisfied.

* The page supports the strategic positioning of Mall Agro.
* The operational context is clearly explained.
* Equipment is introduced through process understanding.
* Information density remains consistently high.
* No unsupported claims are present.
* Terminology follows the Editorial Playbook.
* Internal links support the visitor journey.
* Calls-to-action encourage meaningful project discussions.
* The content is understandable for business decision-makers while remaining credible for engineers.
* The page passes the Editorial QA Checklist.

Editorial completion is achieved when no sentence exists solely to fill space.

Every paragraph should either improve understanding, increase credibility or guide the visitor toward the next stage of the decision-making process.

Every page published by Mall Agro should leave visitors with greater understanding than they had before arriving.

# 28. Common Editorial Mistakes

The following patterns should be avoided throughout the website.

---

## Product-First Thinking

Poor:

> Here are our machines.

Preferred:

> Here is the operational challenge those machines are designed to address.

---

## Feature Lists Without Context

Poor:

* High capacity
* Durable construction
* Reliable performance

Preferred:

Explain why these characteristics matter within the production process.

---

## Repetition

Poor:

> Equipment selection depends on operational requirements.

> Operational requirements determine equipment selection.

> Equipment should be selected according to operational requirements.

Three sentences communicate one idea.

Replace them with one stronger paragraph introducing additional knowledge.

---

## Empty Technical Language

Avoid paragraphs that sound technical without actually explaining anything.

Poor:

> Modern industrial solutions improve operational efficiency through advanced technology.

Preferred:

Explain which production stage improves, why it improves and which operational dependency changes.

---

## Company-Centred Writing

Poor:

* We provide...
* We offer...
* We deliver...
* We specialise...

Preferred:

Focus on:

* the visitor;
* the process;
* the workflow;
* the operational challenge.

The company becomes credible by demonstrating understanding rather than repeatedly describing itself.

---

# 29. Editorial Decision Tree

Whenever uncertainty exists during writing, follow this decision sequence.

**Step 1**

Am I describing the operation?

If **No**, rewrite.

↓

**Step 2**

Am I explaining why equipment exists?

If **No**, rewrite.

↓

**Step 3**

Does every paragraph introduce new knowledge?

If **No**, simplify or replace it.

↓

**Step 4**

Does the reader understand more about the process?

If **No**, improve the explanation.

↓

**Step 5**

Does the content naturally prepare for a project discussion?

If **No**, strengthen the transition.

↓

**Step 6**

Can every public statement be defended?

If **No**, remove or soften the claim.

Only after every answer is **Yes** should the content move to implementation.

# Appendix A — Operational Editorial Vocabulary

This appendix defines the preferred editorial terminology used throughout the Mall Agro platform.

Its purpose is not to create a dictionary, but to ensure that all contributors use industrial language consistently across every page.

Whenever multiple words could describe the same concept, the preferred term listed here should normally be used unless the context requires otherwise.

---

# Workflow

### Definition

The sequence of operational activities required to achieve a production objective.

### Preferred Use

Use when describing:

* production stages;
* operational sequence;
* interaction between equipment;
* industrial processes.

### Avoid Using For

* movement of a single machine;
* physical transportation of equipment.

### Example

> Equipment should support the overall workflow rather than optimise only one production stage.

---

# Operation

### Definition

The practical industrial or agricultural activity performed by the client.

### Preferred Use

Use when referring to:

* farming activities;
* grain handling;
* food production;
* industrial production environments.

### Avoid Using For

Internal business operations.

### Example

> Every operation has different production objectives and equipment requirements.

---

# Process

### Definition

A defined production activity or transformation stage.

### Preferred Use

Use when describing:

* production processes;
* processing stages;
* manufacturing activities;
* agricultural preparation.

### Avoid Using For

Business procedures or administrative tasks.

### Example

> Equipment selection depends on the requirements of the production process.

---

# Material Flow

### Definition

The movement of raw materials or products throughout the production environment.

### Preferred Use

Use when discussing:

* conveyors;
* elevators;
* transport systems;
* storage;
* production integration.

### Avoid Using For

Employee movement.

Vehicle traffic.

### Example

> Material flow often determines the overall equipment strategy.

---

# Equipment

### Definition

Machines, systems or industrial installations supporting an operational objective.

### Preferred Use

General reference to machinery.

### Avoid Using For

Individual branded products unless specifically discussing them.

### Example

> Equipment should be selected according to operational requirements.

---

# Equipment Family

### Definition

A group of equipment serving a similar operational purpose.

### Preferred Use

Instead of listing individual machines early in the discussion.

### Example

> Several equipment families may support this production stage depending on workflow requirements.

---

# Equipment Selection

### Definition

The structured process of determining appropriate equipment.

### Preferred Use

Use throughout category pages.

### Avoid

Product choice.

Machine shopping.

### Example

> Equipment selection should begin with understanding the production workflow.

---

# Operational Context

### Definition

The combination of conditions influencing equipment decisions.

### Includes

* workflow;
* production goals;
* material characteristics;
* capacity;
* site conditions;
* existing infrastructure.

### Example

> Understanding the operational context improves project planning.

---

# Production Environment

### Definition

The physical environment where production activities take place.

### Examples

* agricultural field;
* grain facility;
* food production plant;
* industrial processing line.

# Appendix B — Editorial Patterns & Industrial Knowledge Framework

This appendix provides practical editorial patterns that should be reused throughout the Mall Agro platform.

Its purpose is to ensure that all pages communicate industrial knowledge consistently while maintaining a unified editorial identity.

The patterns below are recommendations rather than mandatory templates. They may be adapted when the operational context requires a different structure.

# 2. Hero Pattern

A strong hero section should answer three questions immediately.

### What operational area is this?

One concise headline.

---

### Why is it important?

One short supporting paragraph introducing the operational context.

---

### What should the visitor do next?

One primary CTA encouraging structured project discussion.

* Discuss Your Project
* Explore Equipment Directions
* Start a Project Discussion
* Request Project Consultation

# 3. Category Introduction Pattern

Every category introduction should follow the same editorial sequence.

Start by explaining:

* the operational area;
* the production objective;
* where the activity fits within the wider workflow.

Only afterwards introduce equipment.

Avoid starting with machinery.

---

Preferred sequence:

```text
Operation

↓

Workflow

↓

Operational Importance

↓

Equipment Role

↓

Selection Factors
```

# 4. Equipment Context Pattern

Equipment should always be introduced through the problem it solves.

Avoid:

> This category includes conveyors.

Prefer:

> Controlled material movement between production stages often requires conveying equipment designed around workflow requirements and production capacity.

The equipment becomes the consequence of the operational need.

---

# 5. Section Opening Pattern

Whenever possible, begin sections by describing the operational objective.

Avoid:

> Various machines are available...

Prefer:

> Efficient grain reception begins with controlled intake, material assessment and reliable transfer into the downstream production workflow.

The reader immediately understands the purpose before reading about equipment.

---

# 6. Operational Question Pattern

Pages should naturally answer practical industrial questions.

Examples include:

* What is the purpose of this operational area?
* Which production stages are involved?
* What challenges typically occur?
* What information influences equipment selection?
* How does this process connect with surrounding operations?
* Which discussions should happen before selecting equipment?

If these questions remain unanswered, the page is probably incomplete.
